Design system audit · 12 August 2026

The tokens are coherent.
The recipes are not.

Kalaanba's token layer is well-organised and genuinely thought through. What fails is the layer above it: four of five filled buttons can't legibly show their own label, the three-tier elevation system resolves to one tier, and the focus ring is invisible on the control it most needs to mark. Everything below is measured, not eyeballed.

Resolved — 12 August 2026

Everything below has been fixed and shipped under WP-20260812-oklch-token-migration / ADR-0006. The findings are kept as written so the reasoning survives; the fix for each is recorded at the end of its card. See /design for the built result.

This is a coherent, product-specific system — ~60 primitives composing one real interaction recipe, not a shadcn reskin. 02 — Measured

What's visually wrong

Six findings, each computed from the shipped token values.

P1

Filled controls can't show their own label

globals.css:29–58 · button.tsx:23–54

Every filled intent pairs a bright fill with a pure-white label. Measured against WCAG AA for normal text (4.5:1), four of five fail — including the primary action. Warning passes only because it uses a near-black label instead.

Primary 3.16:1 Accent 2.21:1 Success 3.30:1 Danger 3.76:1 Warning 8.70:1

The root cause is lightness: the brand colours sit at OKLCH L 0.68 (pink) and L 0.75 (blue). Nothing at that lightness can carry white text. They are excellent ink colours — pink on --surface measures 5.45:1 — and were pressed into service as fills.

Fixed. Fills retuned to L ~0.55. Every intent now clears AA in all three states: primary 5.23 / 4.61 / 6.50.

P1

The elevation system has one tier, not three

card.tsx:25–38 · DESIGN_LANGUAGE §2.4

CardTone exposes flat and raised, and both resolve to the identical string — bg-surface shadow-md. The prop is a no-op. The spec's inset top highlight, described as "what separates this from flat web", appears zero times in the codebase. --surface-overlay is defined and registered with zero usages.

Underneath that, the ramp itself can't support three tiers. --surface → --surface-elev is a lightness step of ΔL 0.022 — roughly half the size of the step below it, and under the threshold where a large flat area reads as separate at all.

Current ΔL +.047 / +.022 / +.041
--bg       L .175
--surface  L .222
--elev     L .244
--overlay L .285

The surface→elev edge is the one that disappears.

Proposed ΔL +.040 / +.040 / +.040
--bg       L .165
--surface  L .205
--elev     L .245
--overlay L .285

Every edge carries the same weight.

Fixed. Ramp evened to a uniform ΔL 0.040. Card rebuilt on .elev-flat / .elev-raised / .elev-floating; inset highlight implemented.

P1

The focus ring is invisible where it matters most

globals.css:76–77 · pressable.tsx:29

--ring is rgba(245,86,148,.35) — pink. On a pink primary button it is pink on pink. WCAG 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for focus indicators; this measures 1.00:1 against its own fill. Keyboard users lose the primary action entirely.

Fixed. --ring moved to its own hue (200). Every focus-visible:ring-* in the suite now resolves to it.

P1

Size classes silently defeat the 44 px floor

button.tsx:57 · chip.tsx:29–30, 100

cn is twMerge(clsx(…)). Because SIZE[size] is composed after pressableBase, tailwind-merge resolves the conflict by keeping the later class — so min-h-9 wins over min-h-11.

PrimitiveDeclaredRendersFloor
Button smh-9 min-h-936 pxFAIL
Chip smh-7 min-h-728 pxFAIL
Chip mdh-8 min-h-832 pxFAIL
Button md / lgh-11 / h-1244 / 48 pxPASS

This is shipping in feature code: the Accept and Decline actions in club-requests-manager.tsx:53–72 are 36 px targets on a phone. §9.1 calls sub-44 a refusal trigger and §9.7 gates QA sign-off on it — so the system's own hard rule is being broken by the system's own primitives.

Fixed. .kx-tap-expand grows the pointer target with a pseudo-element and is deliberately not a Tailwind utility, so twMerge cannot drop it.

P2

Danger and brand are neighbours, not opposites

globals.css:14–19, 29, 41

The token file asserts "danger → red (clearly NOT pink)". Measured, they sit 25° apart at near-identical chroma and lightness:

TokenLCH
--pink0.6820.201359.9°
--danger0.6370.20825.3°

At badge and chip scale on a dark ground, a destructive action and a brand accent are one glance apart.

Fixed. Re-spaced: primary 350, danger 30. Minimum separation across all six semantic roles is now 40°, enforced by a unit test.

P2

One neutral broke the family

globals.css:50

Every dark neutral is hue-consistent at 265–267° — genuinely good discipline. Except --fg-muted: #c0c0c0, which measures C 0.000: a dead neutral grey dropped into a blue-black family. Against a cool ground it reads slightly warm and dirty. The same break exists in the light theme, where --surface: #ffffff is pure neutral among 264° neighbours.

Fixed. All neutrals hue-locked to 264 with non-zero chroma, asserted in design-tokens.test.ts.

P3

Dead weight in the token layer

globals.css · layout.tsx:26–31, 69
  • --radius-button — declared, aliased, registered in @theme inline, used nowhere. Every control is rounded-full.
  • --shadow-pink — declared in both themes, registered, zero usages. Also brand-coupled: it hardcodes the pink RGB, so changing brand breaks it.
  • Archivo is loaded via next/font and attached to <html>, but --font-archivo is referenced nowhere. A variable font with a wdth axis ships on every route for nothing.
  • ~30 lines of --kx-* archive aliases live in the active token file.

Fixed. --radius-button aliased to the pill token, --shadow-pink deleted, Archivo removed from the font load, --radius-tile finally defined.

04 — Proposal

An OKLCH system, tuned for football

The reference points — the Premier League's graphic language and the two dashboards you sent — share one grammar: a deep near-neutral ground, elevation carried by lightness rather than shadow, hairline separators, huge tabular numerals, and one saturated colour doing real signal work while photography carries the warmth.

Why OKLCH earns its place here

Hex can't tell you that #161b26 → #1a202d is half the step of #0b101d → #161b26. OKLCH can, because L is perceptually uniform: equal ΔL means equal perceived step. That single property fixes the elevation bug at the source, makes hover and press a lightness delta instead of a white-mix (which is what currently desaturates pink into chalk on hover), and lets hue be locked across a whole neutral family so an orphan like --fg-muted becomes impossible to introduce.

Ground — hue locked at 264°, uniform ΔL 0.040

bg
--bg0.165 0.018 264
surface
--surface0.205 0.020 264
elev
--surface-elev0.245 0.022 264
overlay
--surface-overlay0.285 0.024 264

Chroma rises slightly with lightness — the way real material behaves as light hits it. It's a small thing that separates a considered dark ramp from a tinted grey one.

The split is the key move: brand-as-fill and brand-as-ink become separate tokens instead of one colour asked to do both jobs badly. --live is a genuine domain semantic — in-play matches — and is the only place the electric colour is allowed to appear. That's the "lively" you asked for, rationed so it still means something.

State without white-mixing

/* Current — mixes toward white, desaturating pink into chalk */
hover: color-mix(in oklab, var(--primary) 86%, white 14%)

/* Proposed — move L only; hue and chroma hold */
--primary:         oklch(0.560 0.200 350)
--primary-hover:   oklch(0.610 0.200 350)   ΔL +0.05
--primary-pressed: oklch(0.510 0.200 350)   ΔL −0.05

Same delta, every colour, every theme. No per-token hand-tuning, and the brand never goes pale on hover.

05 — Shape & type

Why it reads consumer, not sport

The radius scale has no tight end

Everything is either a full pill or 25–32 px. Cards are 1.6rem, hero cards 2rem, and every control is rounded-full. There is no radius for a dense row — so fixture lists, ledger rows, and standings tables have nothing to sit in that doesn't look like a soft consumer card.

Both references use tight geometry for data and generous geometry for panels. Proposed: --radius-row: 10px, --radius-control: 12px, --radius-card: 20px, --radius-panel: 28px, pill reserved for filters and status only.

Numerals are the product, and they have no home

A football platform's core object is a number — a score, a position, a stat line. §2.6 specifies a numeric treatment; nothing implements it. Ten components reach for Tailwind's tabular-nums independently, which means ten chances to forget.

Proposed: a --font-numeric token carrying font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums plus "cv11", and a <StatValue> primitive that owns it. Scores stop being styled per-call-site.

The primitives a football product doesn't have yet

The inventory is strong on generic app furniture and empty on the domain. There is no way to render the thing the product exists to render:

  • ScoreLine — the two-crest, big-numeral result object both references build their whole layout around
  • FixtureRow — dense, tappable, date/venue/status, the atom of every list in the app
  • StandingsTable — tabular numerals, sticky first column, form guide
  • Crest — club identity at consistent scale with a real fallback
  • LiveIndicator — the sole consumer of --live
  • StatValue / Eyebrow — both already specified in §4.1, neither built

Until these exist, every feature screen re-invents them inline, which is where visual uniformity actually goes to die — not in the tokens.
